PULSEPAL CONNECT
Phone-to-Desktop Health Sync - Product Pitch and Implementation Proposal
21 September 2026

Status: Proposed next phase. Phone synchronization is NOT implemented in the
current application. PulsePal's existing working demo uses synthetic data.

===============================================================================
THE PITCH
===============================================================================

Your wearable understands parts of your day. Your phone holds those signals.
Your desktop is where much of your work happens.

PulsePal Connect would bring these experiences together.

Instead of asking people to buy another device or constantly check a health
dashboard, a small PulsePal mobile companion would securely share selected,
user-authorized health records with the PulsePal Windows companion. The desktop
could then combine recent measurements and sleep history with available work
context to offer more informed focus and recovery suggestions.

The experience remains familiar: a quiet tray companion, original personalized
avatars, explainable prompts, focus sessions and practical break choices.
The change is the source of information: from clearly labeled synthetic samples
to clearly labeled, permission-based records from a person's own phone.

Positioning:
"From your wrist, through your phone, to a more considerate workday."

PulsePal's original tagline:
"Your desktop knows what you're doing. PulsePal knows how you're feeling."

The tagline expresses a vision, not a claim that sensor data reveals feelings.
Health measurements provide context, not certainty about a person's mental state.

===============================================================================
1. THE OPPORTUNITY
===============================================================================

Many people already use a wearable connected to a phone. Their health platform
may contain heart-rate records, steps, sleep sessions and other useful signals.
Meanwhile, their desktop productivity experience is usually disconnected from
that context.

PulsePal can explore a useful middle ground:
- Make authorized wellness context available where work happens.
- Encourage a manageable pace after a short night of sleep.
- Offer considerate pauses rather than another stream of interruptions.
- Explain the source and age of every measurement used.
- Let people choose what is shared and stop sharing at any time.

Illustrative interaction:
"Your latest synced sleep record shows a shorter night. Would you prefer a
shorter focus block and a five-minute screen break afterward?"

This is a suggested future experience, not a clinically validated intervention
or a claim that all of its scheduling behavior exists today.

===============================================================================
2. WHY USE THE PHONE AS THE BRIDGE?
===============================================================================

Connecting Windows separately to every watch and ring is difficult. Devices have
different radio interfaces, vendor permissions, APIs and proprietary metrics.

A phone bridge provides a more practical starting point:
- Android Health Connect can expose records written by compatible health apps.
- Samsung Health offers Android integration options for supported records.
- Apple HealthKit exposes authorized health records to an iPhone companion app.
- The desktop can consume a common PulsePal synchronization format.

This reduces the number of device-specific paths, but does not eliminate platform
integration work. A vendor app may display information it does not share through
Health Connect, HealthKit or its SDK. Device and metric support must be verified.

We would read approved platform APIs, not scrape another application's screen,
bypass its permissions, or request the user's health-account password.

3. PROPOSED END-TO-END EXPERIENCE
===============================================================================

  Wearable device
		 |
		 | Existing supported wearable-to-phone synchronization
		 v
  Phone health platform
  Android Health Connect / Samsung Health / Apple HealthKit
		 |
		 | Explicit read permissions for selected data categories
		 v
  PulsePal mobile companion
		 |
		 | Secure paired synchronization
		 v
  PulsePal Windows companion
		 |
		 | Freshness-aware, conservative interpretation
		 v
  Focus and recovery suggestions with explainable reasons

Proposed onboarding:
1. Open Connect phone in PulsePal on Windows.
2. Scan its pairing QR code with the PulsePal mobile companion.
3. Confirm the pairing on both devices.
4. Choose which health categories to share.
5. Tap Sync now to transfer available authorized records.
6. See the source, measurement time and synchronization status on Windows.
7. Disconnect, revoke permissions or delete locally retained records when desired.

Pairing must not grant access to every record automatically. Platform permission
and the user's sharing choices both govern what is transferred.

===============================================================================
4. PLATFORM STRATEGY
===============================================================================

ANDROID - HEALTH CONNECT

Recommended first implementation. Build a small Android companion that reads
selected records from Health Connect with the user's permission.

Start with heart rate, steps and sleep where available. The phone's source health
app must actually write those records into Health Connect. Health Connect is a
record store and interoperability layer, not a universal live sensor stream.

SAMSUNG - AN ANDROID INTEGRATION PATH

Samsung is not a third mobile operating system. For compatible Samsung setups,
start by evaluating Samsung Health's sharing through Health Connect. If required
data is not available there, evaluate Samsung Health Data SDK support.

The SDK offers supported records such as heart rate, sleep, steps and energy
score, subject to device/app support, user permissions and Samsung's SDK access
and distribution requirements. An energy score is not automatically equivalent
to Garmin Body Battery or another vendor's readiness score.

APPLE - IPHONE COMPANION USING HEALTHKIT

An iPhone app can request permission to read supported Apple Health records,
including records originating from Apple Watch. It then relays permitted data
to Windows using the same PulsePal synchronization contract.

There is no general Windows endpoint that directly queries a person's iPhone
HealthKit store. HealthKit and background delivery require native integration.
Development/signing normally requires a Mac with Xcode and Apple tooling.

SHARED CODE, PLATFORM-SPECIFIC ACCESS

.NET MAUI is a possible way to share mobile UI, networking and data contracts.
It does not remove the need for platform-specific Health Connect/HealthKit
bindings, lifecycle handling, permissions and real-device testing. A native
Android-first proof of concept is also reasonable if it reduces integration risk.
The final mobile framework choice should follow a small technical feasibility spike.

===============================================================================
5. SYNCED RECORDS ARE NOT THE SAME AS LIVE STREAMING
===============================================================================

There are three distinct service levels:

Manual synchronization
  The user taps Sync now. The app reads records already available on the phone
  and transfers them. This is the recommended first milestone.

Background synchronization
  New records transfer when the operating system permits background work.
  Timing depends on permission, battery policies, connectivity and source sync.
  It cannot promise a fixed two-second update rate.

Live sensor session
  A supported watch app, BLE sensor or vendor SDK streams current measurements.
  This is a separate future integration, not a capability automatically obtained
  by reading a health store.

Data passes through multiple stages:
  Measurement -> wearable processing -> phone sync -> health-store availability
  -> PulsePal read -> transfer -> desktop receipt.

Each stage can add delay. A record received now may describe a measurement taken
much earlier. The desktop must preserve original measurement time, not stamp
cached data as if it were newly measured.

Last night's sleep can remain useful for today's briefing. A heart-rate reading
from twenty minutes ago must not trigger a claim about stress "right now."

===============================================================================
6. FIRST-PROTOTYPE SCOPE
===============================================================================

Recommended scope:
- One Android phone and one Windows PC.
- Health Connect read access for heart rate, steps and sleep.
- A known compatible source app/wearable combination.
- Foreground Sync now, with clear progress and error messages.
- Secure QR pairing on a trusted, reachable local network.
- Local-only storage with explicit retention and deletion behavior.
- Windows source, measurement-time and stale/disconnected indicators.
- Existing Demo mode retained separately.

Not part of the first prototype:
- Continuous live stress measurement.
- Universal compatibility with every watch and ring.
- Proprietary readiness/stress scores on every platform.
- Cloud hosting or multi-device account synchronization.
- Medical interpretation, diagnosis or emergency detection.
- Real notification interception or productivity monitoring.

The first success is trustworthy data transfer, not maximizing the number of
fields filled in on the desktop.

===============================================================================
7. TRANSPORT OPTIONS
===============================================================================

OPTION A: LOCAL NETWORK - RECOMMENDED FIRST

Phone and Windows communicate directly when they can reach each other on the
local network. No Azure subscription or public backend is required.

Proposed security design:
- Explicit start/stop control for the desktop pairing listener.
- Short-lived, single-use pairing challenge in the QR code.
- User confirmation binding the intended phone and PC.
- Encrypted transport with verified server identity, for example certificate
  pinning anchored by the confirmed pairing process.
- A separate revocable device credential after pairing.
- OS-protected credential/key storage rather than plain-text tokens in JSON.
- Narrowly scoped local-network/firewall access, never an unprotected public port.

We must not simply disable certificate validation to make local HTTPS work.
The QR code must not contain health records or a permanent reusable secret.

Limitations:
- Guest Wi-Fi or corporate networks may block device-to-device traffic.
- Windows may need an explicitly approved firewall exception.
- PC sleep, phone background suspension and network changes interrupt transfer.
- Queuing and retry should not turn old records into supposedly current readings.

OPTION B: OPTIONAL CLOUD RELAY - LATER

A backend can receive authorized records from the phone and deliver them when
the desktop reconnects. This enables different networks and offline buffering.

It also adds user accounts, hosting cost, security operations, data residency,
retention, deletion and incident-response responsibilities. A relay does not
bypass iOS/Android background limits or create measurements the phone lacks.
Azure could host it, but Azure is not required by the concept or local MVP.

===============================================================================
8. DATA CONTRACT AND PROVIDER DESIGN
===============================================================================

PulsePal already has an IWearableProvider boundary. A proposed
PhoneBridgeWearableProvider could expose authorized phone records to the desktop
without replacing the tray, avatars or recovery interfaces.

However, the existing single-sample model needs additional provenance/freshness
support. Sleep, steps and heart rate can have different measurement periods; one
overall sample timestamp is not sufficient to describe every field accurately.

Proposed transfer metadata:
- Schema version and paired device identifier.
- Source platform, source application and device origin where available.
- Stable source-record identifier for deduplication.
- Measurement start/end time and applicable time-zone information.
- Metric type, original units and normalized units.
- Source update/deletion information where the platform supports it.
- Permission/access status, distinguished from zero or "no measurement."
- Desktop receipt time, recorded separately from measurement time.

Implementation needs:
- Incremental synchronization with checkpoints and safe retry.
- Idempotent record ingestion to avoid counting the same steps twice.
- Source-aware aggregation instead of blindly summing overlapping records.
- Bounds and schema validation without inventing missing values.
- Explicit handling of corrections, revoked access and unsupported categories.
- Per-metric freshness policies and visible confidence/availability information.

A missing proprietary score stays unavailable. A PulsePal-derived estimate must
be labeled as such, not displayed as a vendor measurement.

===============================================================================
9. CHANGES TO THE INTELLIGENCE ENGINE
===============================================================================

The current demo deliberately accelerates scenarios and uses synthetic rules to
make stress and recovery easy to demonstrate. These rules should not simply be
connected to real readings unchanged.

Before connected data drives automatic prompts, we must:
- Separate Demo, Replay and Connected modes visibly.
- Preserve measurement age and avoid treating repeated cached samples as new
  evidence of persistent stress.
- Review thresholds and observation windows for actual measurement cadence.
- Account for movement and other explanations for elevated heart rate.
- Establish conservative behavior when data is sparse, stale or missing.
- Avoid claiming psychological stress from heart rate alone.
- Distinguish reported wearable scores from PulsePal estimates.
- Record break completion without modifying actual measurements to show recovery.
- Preserve dismissal, snooze and user control.

For the first connected prototype, display validated measurements and contextual
briefings before enabling stronger automated health-related interpretations.
FocusScore and burnout-risk scores remain heuristics, not medical measurements.

===============================================================================
10. PRIVACY AS A PRODUCT REQUIREMENT
===============================================================================

The proposed bridge should be read-only initially and collect only the data
needed for a chosen feature.

Required controls include:
- Granular permissions and a clear explanation of each category's purpose.
- A visible list of paired devices and last synchronization time.
- Disconnect/revoke controls and deletion of retained PulsePal records.
- Protected credential storage and a reviewed encryption-at-rest strategy.
- Defined retention limits rather than indefinite health-data accumulation.
- Redacted diagnostics: no raw health payloads or tokens in routine logs.
- No advertising use or hidden sharing of health records.
- Honest unavailable/stale states when access is denied or the phone disconnects.

The current demo's plain-text JSON storage is not sufficient evidence of a
production-grade sensitive-health-data storage design. This must be addressed
as part of the connected-data work, not treated as already completed.

The connected product would still state:
"PulsePal is not a medical device."

Synthetic data must retain its demo label. Connected records must instead be
identified accurately by their source and age. There must be no silent fallback
from real data to invented measurements.

===============================================================================
11. PROPOSED DELIVERY PHASES AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
===============================================================================

PHASE 0 - DEVICE AND ACCESS FEASIBILITY
Confirm the actual phone, wearable and source app. Verify which records can be
read through the intended API and how quickly they arrive. Check permissions,
SDK compatibility, distribution rules and development hardware requirements.

Success: a small read-only mobile prototype can display authorized source
records with their original timestamps. No Windows integration is required yet.

PHASE 1 - ANDROID LOCAL SYNC MVP
Implement pairing, manual sync, the desktop bridge provider and freshness UI.
Keep automatic health interpretation conservative and clearly separated from
the existing synthetic demo behavior.

Success: known records appear correctly on Windows; repeated sync does not
duplicate them; disconnection and permission denial are visible; missing fields
stay unavailable; neither secret material nor raw records leak into logs.

PHASE 2 - RELIABLE INCREMENTAL SYNC
Add platform-supported background reads, checkpointing, reconnection, deletion
handling and robust source-aware aggregation.

Success: resume after PC sleep/network loss without duplication or false freshness.
Background timing is documented from actual devices rather than promised in advance.

PHASE 3 - IPHONE / HEALTHKIT
Implement the Apple adapter and phone-to-PC client using the same transfer format.
Validate signing, HealthKit authorization and system-managed background behavior.

Success: Windows consumes Android and iPhone records through the same bridge
contract while preserving platform/source differences.

PHASE 4 - OPTIONAL RICHER INTEGRATIONS
Evaluate Samsung-specific metrics, approved Garmin or other vendor integrations,
live sensor sessions, optional cloud relay and more personalized interpretation.

Success: each capability has verified device support, appropriate permissions
and evidence-based descriptions of its limitations.

No delivery dates are committed in this proposal. Effort depends on the selected
hardware, API access and distribution requirements. Android-first keeps the
initial milestone narrower and reduces dependency on Apple build tooling.

12. PRESENTATION STORY
===============================================================================

Suggested future demonstration:
1. Show the existing offline PulsePal companion and explain Demo mode.
2. Pair an Android phone by scanning the desktop QR code.
3. Grant read permission for selected health categories.
4. Tap Sync now and show actual records arriving on the PC.
5. Point out Measured at, Last synced and Source labels.
6. Show that unsupported metrics are unavailable rather than fabricated.
7. Start a focus session or choose a recovery break in the familiar interface.
8. Disconnect the phone and demonstrate an honest disconnected/stale state.
9. Explain that health history can inform a considerate suggestion without
   pretending to diagnose the person or know their feelings.

Until this work is implemented, this sequence is a proposed demo script, not a
feature that can be launched from the current application.

13. WHAT WE ALREADY HAVE VS. WHAT WE WOULD BUILD
===============================================================================

ALREADY IMPLEMENTED
- Windows tray companion and floating WinUI interface.
- Nova, Lumi and Kairo profiles with original art and saved preferences.
- Synthetic wearable scenarios and mock work context.
- Explainable demo scoring and persistent peak-stress prompts.
- Simulated Attention Shield and focus summaries.
- Breathing, screen, water and movement breaks.
- IWearableProvider architecture, replay provider and future-provider stubs.
- Local demo persistence, tests and self-contained presentation build.

PROPOSED NEW WORK
- Mobile companion applications and platform health permissions.
- Health Connect, Samsung-specific and HealthKit adapters as appropriate.
- Secure pairing, transport and device revocation.
- Source-aware synchronization records and per-metric freshness.
- PhoneBridgeWearableProvider and connected-mode UI.
- Sensitive-data storage, retention and deletion controls.
- Conservative real-data interpretation and real-device validation.

The desktop UI and provider boundary are reusable foundations. They do not mean
that the phone bridge, security model or live-data analysis already exists.

===============================================================================
14. SUMMARY PITCH / PROPOSED NEXT DECISION
===============================================================================

PulsePal Connect would turn the phone into a consent-based bridge between the
health ecosystem a person already uses and the desktop where they work.

We do not need to start with a universal wearable integration or a cloud platform.
We can start with one Android phone, one compatible data source, three useful
record types and a secure local Sync now experience.

The proposed next decision is to select the first phone/wearable combination
and approve a read-only Android Health Connect feasibility prototype.

The goal is simple: real information, honestly represented, delivered through
a companion that helps make room for both focused work and recovery.
